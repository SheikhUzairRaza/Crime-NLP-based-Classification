1. Executive Summary

The Vigilex Crime Classification & Triage Platform is an end-to-end operational intelligence system designed to automate the intake, classification, urgency prioritization, and officer assignment of citizen crime complaints. In municipal policing and cybercrime cells, citizens submit incident reports using diverse, informal, and multilingual natural language (standard English, Roman Urdu, colloquial street vernacular, and SMS shorthand).

To eliminate intake bottlenecks during critical emergency response periods (such as armed robbery or live banking OTP fraud), Vigilex integrates a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3 Transformer spanning 47 distinct criminal categories. This model is paired with a dual-stage multilingual normalizer, a transactional Node.js / Express backend with strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), and an enterprise MySQL 8.4 relational database.

2. Dataset Sourcing & Curation Methodology

Creating an enterprise-grade dataset for 47 crime categories required resolving a fundamental industry bottleneck: authentic police narratives are restricted under legal privacy mandates, while existing public corpora cover only narrow cyber domains (e.g., SMS spam or generic hate speech). To achieve high generalizability across both physical and cybercrimes, a hybrid empirical-compositional methodology was executed.

2.2 Compositional Synthesis & Scenario Diversity

For categories without public incident disclosures (e.g., armed robbery, kidnapping, corporate data breaches, land grabbing, and contraband smuggling), a multi-variable template generation engine was architected:

  • Scenario Breadth: Each category contains between 13 and 29 distinct operational situations (averaging 18+ unique scenarios per class).
  • Combinatorial Permutations: Dynamic slots randomized real-world entities: locations, financial amounts, weaponry types (handguns, knives, rifles), vehicle types, and operating methods.
  • 8 Multi-Perspective Writing Styles: Police formal reports, victim emergency calls, witness sworn statements, social media warnings, incident dispatch logs, emails, news agency briefs, and short 1-line emergency summaries (e.g. "my phone was snatched at gunpoint").
  • Noise Injection (30% Distribution): Synthesized 30% of records with human typing noise: spelling slips, phonetic Roman Urdu variations, SMS colloquialisms, and conversational fillers.

2.3 Dataset Partitioning Structure

The dataset was cleanly structured across two master training/test distributions:

  • crime_final_train.csv (78,357 rows): Internally split into 72,088 training samples and 6,269 validation samples (8% hold-out for early stopping).
  • crime_final_test.csv (14,633 rows): Dedicated test partition where whole scenario templates were held out so that test scenarios were never seen during model training.

3.1 Training Hyperparameters & Convergence

DeBERTa-v3 Numerical Stability Fix (FP32 Precision)
DeBERTa-v3 architectures experience numerical underflow and gradient scaling mismatches (ValueError: Attempting to unscale FP16 gradients) when trained under default FP16 mixed precision. The training pipeline was explicitly configured to use full FP32 precision (fp16=False, bf16=False), ensuring 100% stable gradient updates.

5. Multilingual Normalizer & Production Architecture

5.1 Pre-Classification Normalizer (Groq / Gemini)

Citizen complaints are frequently submitted in Roman Urdu, Hindi, or conversational shorthand. The microservice automatically passes raw text through normalizer.py before tokenization:

Production Normalization Example
Citizen Input: "Meri motorcycle ghar k bahir se 2 bandon ne pistol dikha kar cheen li aur mobile bhi le gaye."
LLM Normalized: "Two armed individuals intercepted me outside my house, brandished a pistol, and forcibly stole my motorcycle and mobile phone."
Predicted Output: Robbery & Armed Snatching (Confidence: 99.1%, Severity: Critical, Urgency: true)

5.2 Three-Tier Architecture & Deployment Stack

  • FastAPI Microservice (Port 8000): PyTorch 2.4, Transformers 4.49, DeBERTa-v3 inference engine running under PM2 daemon.
  • Express Backend (Port 5000): Node.js MVCS architecture, Sequelize ORM, Zod validation, JWT authentication with pessimistic database row locks on refresh tokens.
  • MySQL 8.4 Database: Hosted on AWS EC2, configured with B-Tree unique indices on tracking_id and strict foreign keys.
  • Reverse Proxy & Security: Nginx with Let's Encrypt TLSv1.3 SSL, rate-limiting, and CORS reflection on https://vigilex.duckdns.org.
